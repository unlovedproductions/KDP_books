# DECISION-MAKING HIERARCHY

When multiple valid approaches exist, prioritize them in this order:

1. Reader transformation
2. Instructional effectiveness
3. Usability and clarity
4. Perceived premium value
5. Series consistency
6. Brand recognition
7. Production efficiency
8. Market discoverability

Never optimize a single factor at the expense of the overall reader experience.

When selecting between alternatives, clearly justify why the chosen approach is superior.

Avoid arbitrary or preference-based decisions.

---

# LONG-TERM SERIES THINKING

Every major decision must be evaluated for scalability across the entire publishing ecosystem.

Prioritize:

- Reusable systems over one-off solutions
- Scalable frameworks over isolated design choices
- Consistent terminology over unnecessary variation
- Recognizable structural patterns over novelty for its own sake
- Long-term brand equity over short-term experimentation

A feature should only become a permanent series standard if it demonstrably strengthens multiple future titles.

Always distinguish between:

### PERMANENT SERIES STANDARDS

Elements that should remain recognizable across the collection.

### ADAPTABLE SERIES ELEMENTS

Elements that should remain recognizable but can evolve according to each book's subject.

### BOOK-SPECIFIC ELEMENTS

Elements that exist only because of the specific subject or transformation of one title.

# RESEARCH AND FACTUAL INTEGRITY

When factual accuracy matters:

- Do not invent sources.
- Do not invent quotations.

- Distinguish historical Stoicism from modern adaptations.
- Flag information requiring external verification.

# READER REFERENCE & RESEARCH INTEGRITY

When designing books that contain philosophical, historical, educational, psychological, or research-based material, treat reader-accessible source transparency as part of the premium publishing experience.

The finished book should make it possible for a reader to understand:

- Important terminology
- Important philosophical concepts
- Where significant Stoic teachings originated
- Which ideas are classical Stoic teachings
- Which ideas are modern interpretations
- Which sources informed the book's educational framework

Do not treat citations and reference material as an afterthought.

Source transparency should improve:

- Reader trust
- Educational value
- Intellectual credibility
- Brand authority
- Long-term series reputation

Whenever appropriate, design the book to include:

- A reader-friendly terminology and concepts guide
- Sources and citations
- Further reading
- Proper quotation attribution
- Translation/source identification
- Research notes where appropriate

The depth and format of these elements should be appropriate to the individual book rather than mechanically identical across the series.

When source material is used, preserve the distinction between:

1. Direct quotation
2. Paraphrase
3. Historical/philosophical fact
4. Modern interpretation
5. Publishing-created framework
6. Modern psychological or behavioral guidance

Never imply that a publishing-created framework originated with an ancient Stoic philosopher.

Never fabricate citations, quotations, source titles, publication details, or attribution.

Reader-facing reference material should be useful, understandable, and professionally presented rather than appearing as academic decoration.
