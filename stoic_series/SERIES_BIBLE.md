# The Stoic Practice Series — Series Bible

**Status:** Founding specification. Provisional until formally approved or renamed.  
**Document type:** Series source of truth. Not a manuscript. Not a book MPDS.  
**Audience:** Publishing architect, manuscript AI, designer, editor.  
**Decision hierarchy:** transformation → instructional effectiveness → usability → premium value → series consistency → brand → production → discoverability.

Every major element is classified:

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **PERMANENT** | Must remain recognizable across titles. |
| **ADAPTABLE** | Same function and name family; content/intensity may evolve. |
| **BOOK-SPECIFIC** | Exists only because of one title’s subject. Must not redefine the series. |
| **PROVISIONAL** | Locked for production until you rename/approve. Changing it does not break architecture. |

## 0. Authority and scope

This Bible governs all volumes in *The Stoic Practice Series*.

A book-level **Master Publishing Design Specification (MPDS)** is required before any manuscript is written. The MPDS inherits this Bible. It may not silently override Permanent standards. Exceptions must be named, justified against the decision hierarchy, and classified as Book-Specific.

**Out of scope here:** page-by-page maps, finished chapter prose, cover files, font purchases, Volume 1 manuscript.

## 1. Series identity

### 1.1 Working name — PROVISIONAL

**The Stoic Practice Series**

Renameable without structural change. Do not lock metadata, logo type, or imprint legal name until approved.

### 1.2 Vision — PERMANENT

A recognizable house of premium Stoic practice books that train judgment under pressure—not quote collections, not blank journals, not therapy substitutes.

### 1.3 Mission — PERMANENT

Design each title as a complete, completable training sequence: short classical teaching, precise examination, one practiced behavior, then the next stage.

### 1.4 Core promise — PERMANENT

By the last page, the reader can *pause an impression, split control, choose a reasoned response, and review the day*—in ordinary life, without needing a philosophy seminar.

### 1.5 Reader transformation (series-level) — PERMANENT

| From | To |
| --- | --- |
| Reactive, fused with first impressions | Able to separate impression from judgment |
| Confuses control with wishing | Applies the dichotomy of control in live situations |
| Treats Stoicism as quotes/aesthetics | Treats Stoicism as a daily craft of assent and action |
| Abandons journals after novelty | Completes a structured arc and knows the next volume’s job |

### 1.6 Unique selling proposition — PERMANENT

A **curriculum-grade Stoic workbook series**: classical concepts kept accurate, modern life used only as the practice field, every chapter advancing one transformation stage. Competitors are either (a) popular-Stoic pep books with thin practice, (b) academic primers with no practice, or (c) generic prompted journals with Stoic wallpaper.

### 1.7 Competitive positioning — PERMANENT

| Competitor type | Their offer | Our counter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ryan Holiday–style popular Stoicism | Narrative motivation, maxims | Practice architecture + source discipline |
| Academic Stoicism intros | History and doctrine | Doctrine only as fuel for exercise |
| “Stoic journal” KDP templates | Daily prompts, no progression | Sequenced difficulty, no filler pages |
| CBT workbooks | Clinical protocols | Philosophical training; no diagnosis, no treatment claims |
| Gratitude / wellness journals | Mood logging | Judgment training and virtue in action |

**Category (Amazon):** Self-Help / Personal Transformation and Philosophy / Stoicism (verify live KDP browse nodes at listing). Product type: **medium-content guided workbook** with original instructional text. Do **not** upload as a KDP low-content notebook product. Flag: confirm current low-content vs standard paperback rules and ISBN path before Volume 1 setup.

### 1.8 Primary differentiator — PERMANENT

**Assent training as the spine.** Other series journal feelings or collect virtues. This series trains the moment between impression and agreement.

### 1.9 Reader value proposition — PERMANENT

A physical program the reader can finish in 3–5 weeks of serious use (or slower), that leaves a reusable personal method: Control Split, Assent Pause, Evening Review.

### 1.10 Brand contribution — PERMANENT

Each title must make the next title more desirable *because the method is the same and the problem domain is new*—not because the template was copied with a new subtitle.

### 1.11 Brand pillars — PERMANENT

1. **Accuracy over vibe** — classical Stoicism first; modern framing labeled.
2. **Practice over information** — no lesson without a use.
3. **Progression over volume** — fewer, harder, better pages.
4. **Dignity** — adult tone; no cheerleading; no shame.
5. **Completability** — designed to be finished, not displayed.

### 1.12 Reasoning (strategic)

Assent/control is the only spine that scales across adversity, emotion, character, and relationships without turning every book into “another feelings journal.” Popular Stoicism already owns motivation. Academic Stoicism already owns doctrine. The commercial white space is **accurate + completable practice**. Trade-off: slower manuscript production and stricter quote QC. Series implication: every future title must pass the “would this work if we stripped the Stoic labels?” test—*no*; the Stoic mechanics must be load-bearing.

## 2. Reader architecture

### 2.1 Primary reader — PERMANENT

Adult, roughly 28–55, employed or caregiving, high cognitive load. Has encountered Stoicism via quotes, podcasts, or a popular book. Wants composure and clearer decisions. Distrusts both therapy-speak workbooks and empty journals. Will write by hand if the page earns it.

### 2.2 Secondary reader — ADAPTABLE

- Returning practitioner who wants a tighter drill than a blank notebook.
- Gift buyer purchasing for a stressed partner/friend (must look premium in Look Inside).

Not designed for: adolescents, clinical populations, academic specialists, readers seeking religious instruction.

### 2.3 Pain points

- Mind hijacked by first reactions (anger, rumination, social comparison).
- Knows the quotes; cannot apply them at 4 p.m. on a bad day.
- Journals that ask “how do you feel?” without changing what they *do*.
- Philosophy books that never become behavior.

### 2.4 Desires

- Self-command without numbness.
- A method they can remember under stress.
- Intellectual respect (not dumbed-down).
- Evidence they are actually progressing.

### 2.5 Frustrations

- Fake Stoic quotes and “what you don’t control doesn’t matter” nihilism.
- Workbooks that pad to 200 pages.
- Spiritual bypass dressed as virtue.
- Being told to “just journal” with no standard of quality.

### 2.6 Objections (and architecture answers)

| Objection | Answer in product |
| --- | --- |
| “Stoicism is suppression.” | Emotion volume trains *use* of impressions, not denial. Copy forbids “don’t feel.” |
| “I’ve tried journals.” | Progression + completion markers + rising difficulty. No undated infinite prompt grids. |
| “Too academic.” | Lessons capped; jargon defined once; practice dominates page share. |
| “Too woo / too bro.” | Classical terms, plain English, no hustle gospel, no crystal-adjacent wellness. |
| “Is this therapy?” | Explicit non-clinical boundary in front matter of every title. |

### 2.7 Buying motivations

Look Inside shows: a real lesson, a named method, a page that is clearly *work*, not lined paper. Cover and subtitle signal **practice**, not “365 prompts.”

### 2.8 Emotional needs

Respect. Competence. Relief from self-contempt. A sense of joining a craft, not a fandom.

### 2.9 Behavioral challenges the books must design around

- Starts strong, dies at week two → short chapters, visible arc, mid-book recommitment.
- Intellectualizes instead of practicing → every chapter ends in a live-world charge.
- Performs virtue on the page → prompts that demand concrete incidents, not slogans.
- Avoids painful chapters → difficulty ramp + optional depth lanes, not optional *core*.

### 2.10 Expected outcomes (honest, non-clinical)

The reader can: name an impression; withhold rash assent; sort control; choose one just/temperate/courageous/wise act; review the day without self-laceration.

### 2.11 Completion motivators — PERMANENT

Named milestones, short sessions (20–40 min/chapter), rising skill (not rising word count), a final integration that *uses* prior pages, a clear next-volume invitation that continues the same method.

### 2.12 Abandonment risks → design counters

| Risk | Counter |
| --- | --- |
| Chapter 1 too abstract | Open with a live incident, then name the doctrine |
| Prompt sameness | Prompt taxonomy + anti-duplication register |
| Shame spiral in review | Evening Review rules: facts → judgment → one correction |
| Life interrupt | Re-entry page standard in every title (Adaptable content) |
| “I already know this” | Difficulty markers; later chapters require earlier artifacts |

## 3. Educational philosophy

### 3.1 Model name — PERMANENT

**Impression → Assent → Action → Review (IAAR)**

This is the series learning engine. Every volume applies IAAR to a domain.

### 3.2 Shared transformation stages — PERMANENT

Used inside every book, scaled to that book’s subject:

1. **Orient** — why this domain wrecks judgment.
2. **See** — catch impressions and automatic stories.
3. **Split** — dichotomy of control in *this* domain.
4. **Examine** — test the impression; withhold bad assent.
5. **Choose** — virtue as action, not mood.
6. **Endure/Apply** — repeat under slightly harder conditions.
7. **Review** — evening/period review; extract one correction.
8. **Integrate** — portable rule the reader can state in one sentence.

Volumes may merge stages across chapters but may not skip Split, Examine, Choose, or Review.

### 3.3 Difficulty progression — PERMANENT

| Band | Cognitive | Emotional | Writing load |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I Foundation | Identify, define, sort | Low–moderate | Short lines, checklists |
| II Examination | Causal “when/then” patterns | Moderate | Half-page incidents |
| III Pressure | Apply method mid-stress (recalled or live) | High | Full incident + choice |
| IV Integration | Teach-back + personal rule | Moderate (clarity, not drama) | Tight synthesis |

No book opens at Band III. No book stays at Band I after the first third.

### 3.4 Timebox — ADAPTABLE per title, series default

- Chapter session: 20–40 minutes.
- Full book: 14–28 sessions (not “365 days”).
- Prefer **sequential chapters** over undated perpetual journals.

**Reason:** perpetual journals do not complete; completion is a brand pillar. Trade-off: fewer “year-long companion” keywords. Accept it.

### 3.5 Pedagogy rules — PERMANENT

- Teach the minimum doctrine required for the next exercise.
- One new movable concept per chapter (plus rehearsal of the spine).
- Reinforcement must change *function* (e.g., same Control Split, new domain), not wording.
- No clinical protocols (no diagnosis, no treatment plans, no “heal your trauma” claims).
- CBT parallels may be noted once as *modern cousins*, never as “the Stoics invented CBT” as a slogan without nuance.

### 3.6 Completion state (series)

The reader owns a **personal operating card**: their wording of Control Split, Assent Pause, one virtue test, and Evening Review questions. Later volumes refine the card; they do not replace it.

## 4. Catalog architecture

### 4.1 Founding spine — PROVISIONAL titles, PERMANENT order logic

| Vol | Working title (PROVISIONAL) | Domain | Transformation job | Must not become |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | *Foundations: Judgment, Control, Assent* | The method itself | Install IAAR + three signature tools | A miscellaneous Stoic sampler |
| 2 | *Adversity: Obstacle, Endurance, Reframing* | External blows and setbacks | Apply IAAR when plans break | Toxic positivity / “love the obstacle” cliché |
| 3 | *Emotion: Impressions Under Fire* | Anger, fear, grief-adjacent disturbance | Train pre-emotion and assent delay | Emotion-suppression manual |
| 4 | *Character: Virtues in Ordinary Action* | Wisdom, justice, courage, temperance | Virtue as repeated choice | Personality quiz / virtue cosplay |
| 5 | *Relationships: Duty, Boundaries, Social Virtue* | Roles, conflict, other people’s judgments | *oikeiosis* and justice without doormat ethics | Relationship advice column |

**Order justification:** later volumes presuppose Control Split and Assent Pause. Adversity before Emotion because readers accept “hard events” faster than “my anger is a judgment.” Character after Emotion so virtues are not abstract. Relationships last because other people multiply impressions.

**What would break if reordered:** starting with Relationships without Foundations produces generic conflict journaling. Emotion before Foundations produces mood diaries.

### 4.2 Differentiation rule — PERMANENT

Same skeleton. Different **pressure source**, **signature exercise**, **failure mode**, and **final integration artifact**. If two volumes share >30% prompt functions after renaming nouns, one volume is a clone and must be redesigned.

### 4.3 Expansion lanes (not founding; do not produce yet)

Possible later: death/mortality, work/ambition, wealth/desire, political life, parenting. Each must pass: new domain + new failure mode + IAAR still load-bearing. Do not add a volume that is only “more prompts.”

### 4.4 Reading order

Recommended sequential. Volumes 2–5 must be usable by a reader who finished Vol 1 *or* completed a 4-page “Method Primer” included in those volumes (Adaptable length, Permanent function).

## 5. Master chapter framework

### 5.1 Default chapter sequence — PERMANENT order, ADAPTABLE depth

| Block | Required? | Function | Typical pages (6×9) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Chapter opening | Yes | Title, number, one-line job | 1 |
| B. Verified opening quotation | Yes* | Orient to classical stake | 0.25–0.5 (on opening page) |
| C. Orientation | Yes | What you will be able to *do* after | 0.25–0.5 |
| D. Lesson | Yes | Minimum doctrine | 1–2 |
| E. Core principle | Yes | Named, memorable, ≤40 words | on lesson page or 0.5 |
| F. Worked micro-example | Yes | Author-neutral composite incident | 0.5–1 |
| G. Awareness prompts | Yes | Taxonomy L1 | 1 |
| H. Examination / pattern | Yes | Taxonomy L2–L3 | 1–2 |
| I. Signature exercise | Yes | Book’s named drill applied | 1–2 |
| J. Action / Practice Charge | Yes | Live-world behavior | 1 |
| K. Optional depth lane | No | Band III only when earned | 0–1 |
| L. Chapter close | Yes | Progress Mark + one-sentence takeaway | 0.25–0.5 |

\*If no verified quotation is available for that chapter’s precise job, **omit the quote**. Never paraphrase-as-quote. Never invent. A missing quote is a valid design state.

**Do not use every chapter:** Virtue Assessment (full four-virtue score) — reserve for Character volume and for mid/end checkpoints in other volumes. Daily undated journal dumps. Multiple signature exercises.

### 5.2 Placement rules

- Quote before lesson, never after the exercise (quote orients; it does not decorate the end).
- Lesson before prompts. Never prompt what has not been taught *unless* the prompt is diagnostic for the lesson that immediately follows (max one such reverse pair per book).
- Practice Charge is always last substantive act; Progress Mark is last mark.
- Self-assessment checklists sit after examination, not as chapter openers (except Vol 1 diagnostic pretest in front matter).

### 5.3 Classification

| Element | Class |
| --- | --- |
| Blocks A, C–J, L | PERMANENT |
| Block B (quote) | PERMANENT rule (include-or-omit); selection is BOOK-SPECIFIC |
| Block K | ADAPTABLE |
| Virtue Assessment block | ADAPTABLE (required in Vol 4; checkpoint elsewhere) |
| Chapter count | ADAPTABLE (series default 10–14 body chapters + front/back matter) |

### 5.4 Default body length — PROVISIONAL

**10–12 instructional chapters** + opening diagnostic + closing integration. Target interior **120–180 pages** of *justified* content. If a draft exceeds 180 by repeating functions, cut. If under 120 because stages were skipped, add a missing stage—not lined filler.

## 6. Prompt taxonomy

Six levels. A prompt has exactly one **primary** level. Secondary color is allowed; duplicate primary function is not.

### L1 Awareness

- **Purpose:** Make the impression or situation visible.
- **Cognitive:** Identify, name, locate in time.
- **Emotional:** Low–moderate.
- **Types:** “What happened (facts only)?” “What hit first—image, thought, body?”
- **Placement:** Early chapter; after Orientation/Lesson.
- **Do not use:** Late chapters as the only work; mid-stress application chapters.

### L2 Examination

- **Purpose:** Test the impression; separate description from value-judgment.
- **Cognitive:** Analyze, distinguish, question necessity.
- **Emotional:** Moderate.
- **Types:** “What did you add to the facts?” “What are you treating as unbearable that is only unwanted?”
- **Placement:** After L1 in same chapter.
- **Do not use:** Before the reader has a fact/judgment distinction (teach it in Vol 1 Ch 1–2).

### L3 Pattern recognition

- **Purpose:** See recurrence across incidents.
- **Cognitive:** Compare, cluster, “when X then Y.”
- **Emotional:** Moderate; can sting.
- **Types:** “Where else this week did the same story run?” “What role do you keep casting yourself in?”
- **Placement:** Mid-chapter or mid-book; requires ≥2 prior incidents.
- **Do not use:** Chapter 1; one-off books’ first session.

### L4 Accountability

- **Purpose:** Own the assent and the act, without self-abuse.
- **Cognitive:** Attribute agency accurately (what was up to you).
- **Emotional:** Moderate–high.
- **Types:** “Where did you assent too fast?” “What did you do that was yours to own?”
- **Placement:** After examination; before or inside Practice Charge.
- **Do not use:** As shame fuel; never “what’s wrong with you.”

### L5 Transformation

- **Purpose:** Rehearse a different assent or act on the same material.
- **Cognitive:** Counterfactual + chosen principle.
- **Emotional:** High (identity-adjacent).
- **Types:** “If you withheld assent for ten breaths, what remains?” “What would justice require in the next five minutes of that scene?”
- **Placement:** Later third of chapter; later third of book more often.
- **Do not use:** As the first prompt; as abstract virtue theater with no incident.

### L6 Behavioral commitment (Practice Charge)

- **Purpose:** One observable action or restraint in a defined window.
- **Cognitive:** Specify cue, behavior, timeframe.
- **Emotional:** Moderate (implementation, not catharsis).
- **Types:** “Tomorrow, when [cue], I will [pause/split/act]. I will know I did it if [observable].”
- **Placement:** End of every instructional chapter.
- **Do not use:** Vague “be more Stoic”; multiple charges per chapter.

### 6.1 Anti-duplication system — PERMANENT

1. **Function register:** MPDS lists every prompt’s taxonomy level + function tag (e.g., `L2/fact-vs-value`, `L3/role-recurrence`).
2. **Same tag twice** in one book only if: (a) domain changed, and (b) difficulty band increased, and (c) the second instance requires the first’s written artifact.
3. **Wording clone test:** If two prompts remain equivalent after swapping nouns (boss/partner/traffic), delete one.
4. **Reinforcement vs repetition:** Reusing **Control Split** is reinforcement. Reasking “what can you control?” in five phrasings is repetition. Forbidden.

### 6.2 Writing-space standards (prompts)

- L1: 3–6 short lines or 4–8 labeled fields.
- L2–L3: 8–14 lines or a two-column fact/judgment table.
- L5: 8–12 lines plus a one-line principle.
- L6: structured fields only (cue / action / evidence / when). No essay box.

## 7. Signature series elements

Only elements that train IAAR or completion. No mascots, no sticker culture, no fake gamification.

### 7.1 Control Split — PERMANENT

- **Purpose:** Instant dichotomy of control.
- **Reader action:** Sort the situation into *up to me* / *not up to me* / *mixed (my share)*.
- **Visual:** Two-column + narrow third “my share” strip. Hairline rules. Header label **CONTROL SPLIT**.
- **Frequency:** Taught Vol 1; appears at least once per chapter thereafter when a live situation is present.
- **Function:** Cognitive sorting; reduces futile striving.
- **Evolves:** Domain labels change (illness, insult, other people’s opinions). Structure does not.

### 7.2 Assent Pause — PERMANENT

- **Purpose:** Insert delay before agreement with an impression.
- **Reader action:** Mark the impression in one line; check a pause (breaths/count/physical cue); write the *unassented* remainder.
- **Visual:** Narrow horizontal sequence: Impression → Pause → Remainder. Label **ASSENT PAUSE**.
- **Frequency:** Every chapter that includes a live or recalled trigger (most chapters).
- **Function:** Behavioral interrupt; core differentiator.
- **Evolves:** Cue type (breath, doorframe, phone pickup) may be book-specific; name stays.

### 7.3 Evening Review — PERMANENT

- **Purpose:** Epictetan/Senecan self-examination without Catholic guilt aesthetics.
- **Reader action:** Three fixed questions: Where did I assent too fast? Where did I act in accord with virtue? What is tomorrow’s one correction?
- **Visual:** Recurring three-field block. Label **EVENING REVIEW**.
- **Frequency:** End of each chapter’s day-assignment *or* a dedicated review spread every 2–3 chapters (MPDS chooses one pattern per book and keeps it). Series default: **short review at each chapter close + a fuller review every 3 chapters**.
- **Function:** Memory, accountability, course-correct.
- **Evolves:** Third question’s focus tracks volume domain.

### 7.4 Impression Log — ADAPTABLE

- **Purpose:** Train fact vs added judgment.
- **Reader action:** T-chart: *Impression as it struck me* | *What I can defend as fact*.
- **Visual:** T-chart, label **IMPRESSION LOG**.
- **Frequency:** Heavy in Vol 1 and Vol 3; lighter elsewhere.
- **Function:** Epistemic hygiene.
- **Evolves:** Vol 3 adds bodily/affective row; other vols may omit extra rows.

### 7.5 Practice Charge — PERMANENT

- **Purpose:** L6 commitment.
- **Reader action:** Fill cue / behavior / evidence / window.
- **Visual:** Four labeled fields. Label **PRACTICE CHARGE**.
- **Frequency:** Every instructional chapter, exactly once.
- **Function:** Transfer to life.
- **Evolves:** Charge difficulty tracks band.

### 7.6 Progress Mark — PERMANENT

- **Purpose:** Completion without points theater.
- **Reader action:** Check three boxes: I did the reading. I wrote the incident. I set the charge.
- **Visual:** Three small squares + date line. Footer or chapter close. Label **PROGRESS MARK**.
- **Frequency:** Every instructional chapter.
- **Function:** Honest completion signal; Look Inside proof of structure.
- **Evolves:** Wording of the three checks may add a fourth in Vol 4 (virtue act). Never become stars/streaks.

### 7.7 Virtue Check — ADAPTABLE

- **Purpose:** Four-virtue lens on one incident.
- **Reader action:** Rate or note wisdom / justice / courage / temperance as *expressed or failed* in one scene—not as personality scores.
- **Visual:** Four-cell row. Label **VIRTUE CHECK**.
- **Frequency:** Required Vol 4; 2–3 times in other vols as checkpoint.
- **Function:** Prevents “control” from collapsing into withdrawal.
- **Evolves:** Definitions stay series-standard; scenarios are book-specific.

### 7.8 Method Card — PERMANENT (artifact)

- **Purpose:** Portable end-state.
- **Reader action:** Write personal wording of the four tools (Split, Pause, one virtue test, Review).
- **Visual:** Single page, framed by a simple rule, not a certificate border.
- **Frequency:** Draft mid-book; finalize last instructional chapter. Vols 2–5: revise card, do not restart blank.
- **Function:** Integration + cross-volume continuity.
- **Evolves:** Extra line for volume-specific rule (e.g., “in conflict I will…”).

### 7.9 Re-entry Page — PERMANENT function, ADAPTABLE copy

- **Purpose:** Restart after lapse without shame spiral.
- **Reader action:** Date of return; what broke the chain (facts); one Assent Pause on the lapse itself; next Practice Charge.
- **Placement:** After front-matter how-to; referenced when a Progress Mark is skipped.
- **Frequency:** One page per book.

### 7.10 Forbidden fake signatures

Mood-color wheels as Stoic tools. Fake Latin mottos. Coin icons. “Stoic score” leaderboards. Decorative columns that waste write-in space.

## 8. Philosophy and citation standards

### 8.1 Doctrinal spine (teach accurately) — PERMANENT

Use these as load-bearing ideas. Define in Vol 1; reuse consistently:

| Term | Use this meaning in series copy |
| --- | --- |
| Impression (*phantasia*) | What strikes the mind before we agree |
| Assent (*sunkatathesis*) | The agreement that turns an impression into a believed judgment |
| Dichotomy of control | What is up to us (judgment, impulse, desire/aversion in our power) vs not |
| Preferred indifferents | Health, wealth, reputation: selectable, not good in the moral sense |
| Virtue | The sole good: wisdom, justice, courage, temperance (as excellence of rational agency) |
| Passion / disturbance (*pathos*) | Disobedient, false-value emotion—not all feeling |
| *Prohairesis* | Moral purpose / reasoned choice (Epictetus) |
| *Oikeiosis* | Appropriation/affinity; basis for social duty |
| Fate / providence | Flag as ancient cosmological claim; do not require reader metaphysical assent to complete exercises |

**Modern adaptations** (cognitive distancing, implementation intentions, habit cues) may appear **only** when labeled: *Modern practice note* — not as “Marcus said.”

### 8.2 Historical vs modern — PERMANENT

- Classical: what is attested in surviving Stoic texts and standard scholarly consensus.
- Interpretation: what we infer for practice.
- Adaptation: what we add for a 21st-century workbook.

Manuscript must not collapse these three.

**Do not claim:** Stoicism is CBT; Stoics were atheists (they were not, generally); “the obstacle is the way” as a primary-source quotation; that amor fati is a Stoic technical term in the early sources (Nietzschean; may be used only as modern label if explicitly sourced).

### 8.3 Approved source families (for quotation)

Primary (prefer):

- Epictetus, *Discourses*, *Enchiridion* (Arrian)
- Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* (*Ta eis heauton*)
- Seneca, *Epistulae Morales*, *De Ira*, *De Tranquillitate Animi*, other essays
- Fragments of early Stoics via Long & Sedley / standard collections—**use sparingly; verify**

Standard English translations to prefer (pick one per book and stay consistent; list on copyright page):

- Epictetus: Robin Hard (Oxford) or Oldfather (Loeb; public domain US for older Loeb—**verify copyright year**)
- Marcus: Robin Hard (Oxford), or Farquharson; Hays is readable but looser—if used, do not lean on distinctive Hays phrasing as “the Greek”
- Seneca: Graver & Long (Chicago) or Gummere (Loeb; PD status **verify**)

**PROVISIONAL default for the series:** Hard (Epictetus + Marcus) + Graver & Long (Seneca), unless licensing/cost forces PD Loeb/public-domain—then verify US PD status before quoting.

Secondary (may cite in “further reading,” not as ancient voice): Hadot, Long, Graver, Brennan, Sellars, Inwood.

Popular modern (Holiday, Irvine, Robertson): **back matter only**, never mixed into ancient quote blocks.

### 8.4 Quotation rules — PERMANENT

1. No invented quotations. No “Stoic proverb” unsourced.
2. No paraphrase inside quotation marks.
3. Every quote block includes: author, work, book/letter/section identifier, translator, year.
4. If verification cannot be finished in manuscript pass, mark `[[VERIFY QUOTE]]` and do not typeset as final.
5. One quote per chapter opening maximum. Additional quotes only if they *change* the exercise, not decorate.
6. Epigraphs must match the chapter’s job. Decorative wisdom is filler—delete.
7. Public-domain status and translator copyright are **production verification items**. Do not assume.

### 8.5 Placement strategy

- Chapter open: one short verified passage (1–4 sentences).
- Lesson body: paraphrase + citation pointer preferred over long extracts (fair use / licensing hygiene).
- Never place a quote on a write-in page such that it steals line count without teaching.

### 8.6 Citation format (interior)

> Text of quotation.  
> — Author, *Work* X.Y, trans. Name (Year)

Copyright page: full bibliographic list of translations used.

## 9. Editorial style guide

### 9.1 Brand voice — PERMANENT

Calm, exact, adult, slightly stern, never cruel. Teacher who respects the reader’s time. Think: competent coach + responsible classicist, not influencer and not professor performing footnotes.

### 9.2 Tone

- Direct address: *you*.
- Inclusive *we* only when stating shared human patterns (“we assent faster when tired”).
- No hype (“life-changing!!!”, “unlock”, “hack”).
- No mockery of the reader’s past attempts.
- No bro-Stoic contempt for emotion or for other people.

### 9.3 Reading level

Aim **US grade 8–10** for instructions and prompts. Lesson passages may rise to 11–12 when defining a technical term, then return. Define every Greek/Latin term at first use; after that, English preferred with occasional term in parentheses.

### 9.4 Sentence style

- Prefer short and medium sentences. One idea per sentence in instructions.
- Lesson: mix; allow one longer periodic sentence when precision needs it.
- No stacked rhetorical questions.
- No filler transitions (“In today’s fast-paced world…”).

### 9.5 Prompt style

- Imperative or direct question. Not “maybe you could explore…”
- Demand a *particular* incident when the level is L2+.
- Include a constraint (“facts only,” “one scene,” “ten minutes tomorrow”).
- Never double-barreled questions.

### 9.6 Instruction style

Numbered steps when sequence matters. Bold the action verb. State when to stop writing.

### 9.7 Question style

Open enough to be personal, closed enough to be answerable in the space given. If the space is six lines, the question must not ask for a life story.

### 9.8 Encouragement style

Acknowledge difficulty in one clause, then return to the task. No pep paragraphs. After hard chapters: one sentence normalizing struggle + the next concrete step.

### 9.9 Vocabulary standards

**Prefer:** impression, assent, judgment, impulse, virtue, preferred indifferent, up to you / not up to you, role, correction, review.  
**Accept once defined:** *prohairesis*, *phantasia*, *pathos*, *oikeiosis*.  
**Psychological terms allowed:** habit, cue, rumination, trigger (non-clinical), pattern.  
**Psychological terms forbidden as claims:** trauma processing, diagnosis, disorder, therapy, healing your inner child, nervous-system hacks presented as Stoic doctrine.

### 9.10 Terms to avoid

Manifest, vibe, toxic (unless quoting the reader’s own language in an examination prompt), grindset, sigma, “high value,” “feminine/masculine energy,” universe will provide, “good vibes,” “let it go” as a standalone instruction, “what you don’t control doesn’t matter,” “never feel anger.”

### 9.11 Tone boundaries

- Not medical advice. Front-matter disclaimer every title.
- Not political recruitment.
- Not religious conversion. Ancient theology may be described; reader need not share it to complete the book.
- Not anti-therapy. Therapy can be recommended as a parallel professional path when distress is severe—one sentence, no protocol.

### 9.12 Consistency rules

- Dichotomy of control language: always **up to you / not up to you / your share**. Not “circle of control” (Covey bleed).
- Four virtues in this order always: **wisdom, justice, courage, temperance**.
- Series name, tool names, and labels in small caps or consistent header styling: CONTROL SPLIT, ASSENT PAUSE, EVENING REVIEW, IMPRESSION LOG, PRACTICE CHARGE, PROGRESS MARK, VIRTUE CHECK, METHOD CARD.
- Chapter titles: sentence case or title case—**pick title case** as series standard. No clever clickbait titles.

## 10. Visual and production system

### 10.1 Format — PERMANENT defaults

| Spec | Series standard | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trim | **6″ × 9″** (152.4 × 228.6 mm) | KDP default regular trim; most common US trade. Verify live table on [KDP Print Options](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834180). |
| Binding | Paperback first | Hardcover optional later; hardcover min page count is higher (verify). |

| Bleed (interior) | **No bleed** | Simpler, safer. Full-bleed art not part of the system. If a future title bleeds, follow KDP: +0.125″ width, +0.25″ height; outside margin min 0.375″. |
| Target pages | **120–180** useful pages | Must stay within KDP min/max for 6×9 B&W (commonly 24–828 paperback—**verify current chart**). |

### 10.2 Margins and gutter — PERMANENT policy, numbers flagged

KDP **minimums** (no bleed), from KDP interior specs / help (verify before lock):

| Page count | Inside (gutter) min | Outside/top/bottom min (no bleed) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 24–150 | 0.375″ | 0.25″ |
| Higher bands | Increases (e.g. much thicker books up to 0.875″ inside) | Outside no-bleed remains ≥0.25″ |

**Series standard exceeds minima** because these are write-in books (thumb room + pen clearance):

| Page count | Inside | Outside | Top | Bottom |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 24–150 | **0.75″** | **0.75″** | **0.70″** | **0.75″** |
| 151–300 | **0.875″** | **0.75″** | **0.70″** | **0.75″** |

Use **mirror margins**. Single pages, not spreads.  
**VERIFY** against [Set Trim Size, Bleed, and Margins](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834180) / interior specifications before file lock. If KDP minima rise, raise ours to stay ≥0.125″ above min.

### 10.3 Typography — PERMANENT categories, PROVISIONAL faces

**Categories (Permanent):**

| Role | Category | Size / leading (6×9) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chapter number | Small sans, tracked | 9–10 pt |
| Chapter title | Display serif | 18–22 pt |
| Section label (CONTROL SPLIT etc.) | Sans, small caps or tracked caps | 8.5–9.5 pt |
| Lesson body | Text serif | **11 / 15** |
| Prompts | Sans or same serif at 10.5–11 | 10.5 / 14.5 |
| Instructions | Sans | 10 / 13.5 |
| Quotations | Serif italic, slightly smaller | 10.5 / 14 |
| Captions / running footer | Sans | 8–8.5 pt |
| Writing lines | Light rules | 18–20 pt baseline rhythm |

KDP absolute minimum text size is **7 pt**. We never approach it except possibly page numbers.

**Provisional type stack (open/licensed; swap only as a set):**

- Body: Source Serif 4 **or** Literata  
- Display / chapter titles / quotes: EB Garamond **or** Cormorant Garamond  
- UI / labels / instructions: Source Sans 3  

**Forbidden:** Inter, Roboto, Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman as the *brand* stack, Comic fonts, script fonts for body, fake letterpress distress.

Embed fonts in PDF. Confirm commercial print embedding licenses before final.

Do not adopt Amazon Endure as brand voice; it is a KDP page-count font, not this series’ identity.

### 10.4 Hierarchy and spacing

- One H1 equivalent per chapter (title).  
- H2 = block labels (Lesson, Examination, Practice Charge).  
- No H3 deep trees.  
- Consistent space before labels: ~12–14 pt; after labels: ~6–8 pt.  
- Between major blocks: ~16–20 pt.  
- White space is a *tool for writing*, not decoration. Empty lined pages without a prompt function are forbidden.

### 10.5 Writing space

- Ruled lines at 18–20 pt. Line weight hairline (~0.25–0.3 pt) so scans stay clean.  
- Boxes for Control Split: min 1.6″ usable height per column.  
- Checkboxes 10–11 pt square, not tiny.  
- Keep writing area out of the gutter by the inside margin above.  
- Do not put essential rules or prompts in the outer 0.3″ near trim (print variance).

### 10.6 Icons and symbols — PERMANENT restraint

A **mark system**, not an icon pack:

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Solid rule + small caps label | Signature tool block |
| Square checkbox | Progress Mark / binary check |
| Vertical hairline T-chart | Impression Log / Control Split |
| Arrow sequence (simple) | Assent Pause steps |

No skeuomorphic quills, no marble busts as dingbats, no gradient orbs (they fail in B&W). Marks must survive photocopy and 300 dpi print.

### 10.7 Chapter dividers

New chapter always starts **recto** (right-hand page). Previous chapter may end with a blank verso only if needed to force recto start—and only if that verso is truly blank or carries a single quiet series rule (not a fake quote). Prefer tightening previous chapter to avoid empty versos. **Blank pages for “premium feel” are not a standard.**

### 10.8 Callouts

Two callout types only:

1. **Principle** — core principle, max 40 words, left rule 1 pt.  
2. **Modern practice note** — clearly labeled; 3–5 lines max.

No tip bubbles, no “pro tip,” no sticky-note graphics.

### 10.9 Page rhythm

Lesson (denser type) → tool block (structured) → write-in (lighter) → charge (structured). Alternate density so the reader never faces four consecutive heavy text pages or four consecutive empty grids.

### 10.10 Navigation

- Running footer: verso = series working name (short) + author/imprint; recto = short book title + page number.  
- Headers: optional chapter short title on recto after chapter 1. Must **exactly match** KDP title metadata rules for book title/author on title page and any header that repeats them ([KDP template guidance](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834230)).  
- Page numbers: bottom outside or centered bottom—pick **bottom outside** as standard. Front matter: roman numerals. Body: arabic from first instructional page or from title page—**MPDS locks one**; series default: **arabic from half-title/title through**, simpler for workbooks; if using roman front matter, TOC must match.  
- **PROVISIONAL pagination default:** roman front matter; arabic body starting at first chapter. TOC uses arabic body numbers.

### 10.11 B&W print usability

- No gray fills below 8% or above 85% for large areas (mud and banding). Prefer 10–15% only for rare principle bands, or none. **Series preference: no gray fills; rules and type only.**  
- No color-dependent meaning.  
- Screenshots/photos discouraged. If used: 300 DPI grayscale, sufficient contrast.  
- Test photocopy: all labels still readable.

### 10.12 File preparation — PERMANENT process

- Final interior: PDF, fonts embedded, single pages, correct trim (6.00 × 9.00 in, no bleed).  
- Transparency flattened if required by printer.  
- No printer marks in customer file.  
- Cover: wrap PDF via KDP calculator (spine width depends on final page count + paper type). Recalc after pagination lock.  
- Order a **proof copy** before publishing ([KDP proof guidance](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834180)).  
- Remove all placeholder/template text or KDP will reject.

### 10.13 KDP items to re-verify at each production

Flag as `[[VERIFY KDP]]`:

- 6×9 page-count min/max by paper type  
- Margin table by page count  
- Low-content vs standard paperback classification  
- Series feature / reading-order setup  
- Public domain / translation copyright  
- Metadata: title, subtitle, series name consistency  
- Look Inside shows a lesson + a tool, not only lined paper  

Sources used for this Bible: KDP Print Options (G201834180), paperback formatting/templates (G201834190, G201834230), Context7 library `/websites/kdp_amazon_en_us_help` (margins, bleed, file specs). Re-query before Volume 1 file lock.

## 11. Cross-selling and brand ecosystem

### 11.1 Front matter (every title) — PERMANENT order

1. Half-title (book title only)  
2. Series page (list of volumes; “also in this series”)  
3. Title page  
4. Copyright + translation credits + disclaimer (not therapy; verify quotes policy)  
5. How to use this book (2–3 pages: IAAR, tools, timebox, Re-entry)  
6. Method Primer — **full in Vol 1**; **condensed 2–4 pages in Vols 2–5**  
7. Contents  
8. Optional diagnostic pretest (Vol 1 required; others optional)

No dedication required. No fake testimonials.

### 11.2 Back matter — PERMANENT functions, ADAPTABLE copy

1. Final Method Card (if not last chapter)  
2. Further reading (primary sources first, then scholarly, then 1–2 honest popular titles)  
3. Series collection page with **one-line job** per volume (not blurbs stuffed with keywords)  
4. Next-volume bridge: what skill you now have → what problem the next book attacks  
5. About the author/imprint (short, no guru myth)  
6. Notes / citation list if not handled on copyright page

### 11.3 Series introduction

Vol 1 carries the full series promise. Later vols: half-page “If you are new” + pointer to Method Primer. Do not reprint Vol 1’s entire philosophy essay.

### 11.4 Completion-to-next-book transition

Last chapter’s final page:  
- State the portable skill gained.  
- Name the next volume’s **failure mode** (e.g., “this method still fails when the blow is external and unfair—that is Volume 2”).  
- No coupon voice. No “if you loved this book.”

### 11.5 Cross-sell rules — PERMANENT

- Cross-sell only **after** a completed arc, plus series page up front for browsers.  
- Never interrupt a chapter with a sales callout.  
- Never fake “Volume 7” that does not exist. List only published + “in preparation” if real.  
- Amazon series page / KDP series reading order must match the Bible spine ([Start a Book Series](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200652170) — verify current UI).

### 11.6 Reader retention

The Method Card is the retention object. Later books ask the reader to transcribe or amend it. That is the loyalty loop—not email gates (none assumed; no paid stack).

## 12. Quality-control system

Measurable tests. Fail = do not send to typesetting.

| Standard | Test |
| --- | --- |
| No filler | Every page has a stated purpose in the MPDS page map. Delete any page whose purpose is “space,” “breathing room,” or “page count.” |
| No duplicate concepts | Concept register: each doctrine taught once as *new*, then only applied. Second “what is the dichotomy?” lesson = fail. |
| No duplicate prompts | Function-tag collision without domain+band upgrade = fail. Wording-clone test = fail. |
| No weak chapters | Chapter missing any of: lesson objective, L2+, signature tool use, Practice Charge = fail. |
| No dead-end chapters | Next chapter must require a prior artifact or skill. If removable with no damage, it is dead—cut or reconnect. |
| Difficulty progression | Plot chapter bands. Must not decrease across the book except one optional recovery chapter after the hardest. |
| Balanced workload | No chapter >45 min honest estimate; no chapter <12 min unless a designated review spread. Writing-line count within ±30% of series median after Ch 1–2. |
| Logical progression | IAAR stages present; Split before advanced emotion/relationship work. |
| Reader transformation | Pre/post diagnostic items map to the same 4–6 behaviors. If post cannot be answered using the book’s tools, architecture failed. |
| Philosophical accuracy | Spot-check: no modern slogan attributed to ancients; *pathos* not equated with “all emotion”; preferred indifferents not called “evil.” |
| Quote accuracy | Every quote has work+section+translator. Unverified quotes remaining = fail. |
| Brand consistency | Tool labels exact; virtue order exact; voice matches §9. |
| Visual consistency | Only approved marks/callouts; B&W contrast check; margins ≥ series standard. |
| Production quality | KDP previewer clean; fonts embedded; title match; no template junk; proof copy reviewed. |
| Page-purpose integrity | MPDS page map ↔ manuscript 1:1. Extra pages unauthorized. |
| Natural cross-selling | Series page + end bridge only. Mid-chapter CTAs = fail. |

**Philosophical accuracy extra test:** A competent reader of Epictetus/Marcus/Seneca should not find a chapter that teaches the opposite of the cited passage.

**Completion test:** A beta reader can explain Control Split, Assent Pause, and Evening Review without looking them up.

## 16. Design rationale (institutional memory)

| Decision | Reason | Reader benefit | Alternative considered | Trade-off | Series implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IAAR spine | Only model that scales across domains without becoming a mood diary | Rememberable method under stress | Virtue-of-the-week; daily undated journal | Less “365 journal” keyword coverage | All vols share engine |
| Assent as differentiator | Historically central; commercially underserved vs quote books | Distinct skill, not generic journaling | Gratitude + Stoic quotes | Harder to write; easier to do badly | QC must police fake Stoicism |
| 10–12 chapters not 52 weeks | Completability > calendar gimmick | Finishable; higher perceived value | 12-week dated diary | Fewer pages of habitual logging | Reviews will mention “program” not “notebook” |
| Tools named and repeated | Recognition = brand + skill automaticity | Lower cognitive load | New cute names per book | Risk of rigidity | Names are Permanent |
| Exceed KDP margin minima | Write-in + print variance | Usable gutter | Text-book 0.5″ margins | Slightly fewer characters/page | All interiors feel like workbooks |
| No interior bleed / no gray slabs | B&W reliability, cost, speed | Cleaner pages, fewer print defects | Designer-heavy gray fields | Less “magazine” look | Premium via type and structure |
| Quotes omit-if-unverified | Integrity > decoration | Trust | Using famous misattributions | Some chapters without epigraphs | House reputation |
| Vol order Foundations→…→Relationships | Prereq logic | Later books actually usable | Lead with emotions (bigger keyword) | Slower grab of “anxiety” search | Primer required in later vols |
| Medium-content not low-content | Original curriculum; series features; ISBN path | Taken seriously as a book | Low-content upload | More production work | Do not flood catalog with prompt clones |
