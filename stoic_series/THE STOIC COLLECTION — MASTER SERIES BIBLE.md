# 1. SERIES EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**The Stoic Collection** is a premium catalog of guided Stoic workbooks and journals that function as completable transformation programs—not quote anthologies, not blank journals, not mystical “shadow” or wellness products with Stoic wallpaper.

Each book:

- Teaches the minimum classical Stoic doctrine required for the next practice.
- Moves the reader through a recognizable progression of examination → responsibility → virtue-informed action → review.
- Maintains source integrity (no fabricated quotes; classical vs modern clearly labeled).
- Includes reader-facing reference systems appropriate to the title.
- Shares brand voice, visual language, signature frameworks, and terminology.
- Remains distinct through unique problem domain, failure mode, signature exercises, and integration artifact.

**Series differentiator:** Accurate Stoic mechanics as load-bearing structure + instructional design that readers finish.

**Commercial posture:** Medium-content guided workbook / journal on Amazon KDP; premium perceived value; series retention through method continuity and distinct domains.

# 5. EDITORIAL DESIGN SYSTEM

### 5.1 Reading & prose standards — PERMANENT

- **Reading level:** Accessible adult (approx. grades 8–10 clarity) with adult conceptual depth.
- **Sentence length:** Prefer ≤25 words average; vary rhythm.
- **Paragraph length:** Short; scannable on 6×9.
- **Instruction style:** Imperative + specific fields (“Write the facts of one incident from this week.”).
- **Prompt style:** One psychological function per prompt; concrete incident required unless diagnostic.
- **Reflection style:** Facts → judgment → ownership → next act.
- **Challenge style:** Observable behavior in a defined window.
- **Assessment language:** Descriptive, not diagnostic (“Where did temperance fail in this scene?” not “You have an impulse-control disorder.”).
- **Encouragement language:** Respect competence; acknowledge difficulty; forbid empty praise.

### 5.2 Conventions — PERMANENT

- **Capitalization:** Signature element names in small caps or all caps labels as specified in Visual Identity (e.g., PRACTICE COMMITMENT). Stoic virtues lowercase in running text unless titled.
- **Numbers:** Numerals for times, page counts, scales; words for one–nine in prose when not data.
- **Philosophical terms:** Prefer series glossary forms (see §9). Italicize transliterated Greek on first use per book.
- **Psychological terms:** Use sparingly; never as clinical claims. Prefer “pattern,” “reaction,” “avoidance,” “judgment.”
- **Preferred vocabulary:** examine, judgment, assent, impression, responsibility, virtue, character, discipline, practice, standard.
- **Discouraged/prohibited:** heal your inner child; trauma processing (as clinical promise); manifestation; toxic positivity; “the universe”; unsupported “studies show” without citation pathway; “Stoic proverb” unsourced.

### 5.3 Editorial fingerprint — PERMANENT

A random interior page should feel: structured, spare, adult, Stoic-term-literate, write-in-forward, free of motivational poster copy.

# 7. STOIC SOURCE & QUOTE SYSTEM

### 7.1 Source hierarchy — PERMANENT

1. **Primary Stoic texts** (preferred for doctrine and quotes): Epictetus (*Discourses*, *Enchiridion*); Marcus Aurelius (*Meditations*); Seneca (*Letters*, essays).
2. **Early Stoic fragments** via standard scholarly collections (e.g., Long & Sedley)—use sparingly; verify.
3. **Scholarly secondary sources** for historical claims and concept definitions (Hadot, Long, Graver, Brennan, Sellars, Inwood, etc.).
4. **Modern practice literature** (Robertson, Irvine, Holiday, etc.): Further Reading / modern notes only—**never** inside ancient quote blocks.
5. **Quotation websites / unsourced internet collections:** **Not authoritative.** May only be used as leads for verification against primary/scholarly sources.

### 7.2 Translation policy — PERMANENT

- Pick **one consistent translation set per book**; list on copyright page.
- Series default preference (unless licensing forces otherwise): Hard (Epictetus/Marcus) + Graver & Long (Seneca), **or** verified public-domain Loeb/older translations with copyright status checked. `[[VERIFY COPYRIGHT]]`
- Identify translator and year for every direct quotation.

### 7.3 Quotation rules — PERMANENT

1. Never invent quotations.
2. Never put paraphrase in quotation marks.
3. Every direct quote: author, work, section identifier, translator, year.
4. If unverified at manuscript time: mark **REQUIRES SOURCE VERIFICATION** / `[[VERIFY QUOTE]]`—do not typeset as final.
5. Popular “Stoic quotes” of doubtful authenticity: omit or paraphrase as modern sentiment with no ancient attribution.
6. Max one opening quote per chapter; omit if no verified fit. Missing quote is a valid design state.
7. Epigraphs must serve the chapter’s job—not decorate.

### 7.4 Attribution format (interior) — PERMANENT

> Quotation text.  
> — Author, *Work* X.Y, trans. Name (Year)

### 7.5 Paraphrase & claim rules — PERMANENT

- Paraphrase classical ideas in plain English + citation pointer when load-bearing.
- Distinguish: direct quotation | paraphrase | historical fact | modern interpretation | publishing framework | modern behavioral guidance.
- Never attribute publishing frameworks (prompt systems, audits, cards) to ancient authors.

# 10. SOURCES, CITATIONS & FURTHER READING STANDARDS

### 10.1 Citation methodology — PERMANENT

**Hybrid accessible scholarly style:**

- In-body quotes use the attribution format in §7.4.
- Load-bearing paraphrases may use endnote-style markers: superscript numbers or bracketed keys `[S1]`.
- **Sources & Citations** lists full bibliographic entries ordered by key/number.
- Copyright page lists translation editions used.

**Minimum bibliographic fields:** Author; Work/Title; Translator (if any); Editor (if any); Publisher; Year; Section/page identifiers as applicable.

### 10.2 What must be citAble — PERMANENT

- Direct quotations  
- Specific historical claims  
- Distinctive doctrinal formulations presented as classical fact  
- Any modern research claim (rare; must be real and verified)

### 10.3 Further Reading standards — PERMANENT rules / ADAPTABLE list

**Include when:** book teaches doctrine beyond a thin primer OR success criteria include continued study (default for Collection).

**Selection criteria:**

- Prefer primary texts in recommended translations  
- Prefer reputable scholarly intros  
- Modern practice books only in a clearly labeled “Modern practice” subsection  
- Quality over volume (typically 6–12 total recommendations)

**Annotation:** One line per item stating *why* it helps after *this* book.

**Do not:** pad with unread/unverified titles; mix modern authors into primary-source lists.

### 10.4 Verification flag — PERMANENT

Anything not verified at architecture time: **REQUIRES SOURCE VERIFICATION**.

# 14. PROMPT TAXONOMY

A prompt has exactly one **primary** level. Secondary color allowed; duplicate primary function forbidden without escalation rules.

| Level | Name | Purpose | Cognitive | Emotional | Placement | Do not use when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | Awareness | Make situation/impression visible | Identify | Low–mod | Early chapter | As sole late-book work |
| L2 | Examination | Test judgment; facts vs added story | Analyze | Moderate | After L1 | Before fact/judgment taught |
| L3 | Pattern recognition | See recurrence | Compare | Mod–sting | Mid chapter/book | Chapter 1 first session |
| L4 | Accountability | Own assent/act without self-abuse | Attribute agency | Mod–high | After exam | As shame fuel |
| L5 | Transformation | Rehearse different assent/act | Counterfactual + principle | High | Later third | As first prompt / abstract virtue theater |
| L6 | Behavioral commitment | Observable act in window | Specify implementation | Moderate | Chapter end | Vague “be more Stoic” |
| L7* | Integration | Portable rule / standard | Synthesize | Clarity | Late book | Early chapters |

\*L7 is series-permanent for closing architecture; not used as routine mid-chapter spam.

### 14.1 Anti-duplication — PERMANENT

1. MDS maintains a **function register** (level + function tag).  
2. Same tag twice only if domain changed **and** difficulty rose **and** second instance requires prior written artifact.  
3. Wording-clone test: if prompts stay equivalent after noun swap, delete one.  
4. Reusing **Control Sort** across domains = reinforcement. Reasking “what can you control?” five ways = forbidden repetition.

### 14.2 Writing-space defaults — PERMANENT

- L1: 3–6 short lines or 4–8 fields  
- L2–L3: 8–14 lines or structured tables  
- L5: 8–12 lines + one-line principle  
- L6: structured fields only

### 15.2 Difficulty bands — PERMANENT

| IV Integration | Teach-back + personal standard | Clarity-focused | Tight synthesis |

### 15.3 Timebox — ADAPTABLE defaults

- Prefer sequential chapters over perpetual journals  

### 15.4 Pedagogy rules — PERMANENT

- White space protects handwriting; do not fill with ornaments  
- Hairline dividers > heavy boxes stacking  

### 16.5 Icons/symbols — ADAPTABLE within restraint

Simple geometric markers for Progress Mark squares; optional small chapter ornaments if they do not cost write-in space. No ornate crests.

### 16.6 Navigation — PERMANENT

- Arabic pagination in body  
- Roman front matter  
- Running footer: short book title left / page number center or outside / optional series mark  
- Chapter opens on recto when feasible  

### 16.7 B&W print — PERMANENT

Design for 6×9 KDP B&W. No critical info in color. Screens/tints only if they survive B&W clearly (prefer rules/labels over gray fills).

# 24. SERIES GOVERNANCE

### 24.1 Change control — PERMANENT

No individual book may silently redefine a Permanent standard.

Proposed changes must be labeled:

**SERIES-LEVEL CHANGE**

and must state: reason, decision-hierarchy justification, impact on existing titles, migration plan.

### 24.2 Adding a Permanent framework

Allowed only if it strengthens ≥2 future titles and passes: reader value, non-duplication, visual feasibility, editorial fit.

### 24.3 Retiring a framework

Requires Series Bible revision note: why, replacement, which books grandfathered.

### 24.4 Terminology / visual / citation updates

Version the Bible (1.1, 1.2…). Record date + summary changelog at top in future revisions.

### 24.5 Exceptions

Book MDS may list **Book-Specific exceptions** with explicit trade-offs. They do not alter Permanent defaults.

### 24.6 Conflict resolution order

1. MASTER AI ROLE PROMPT  
2. This MASTER SERIES BIBLE  
3. Book Project Brief  
4. MDS Prompt  
5. Book MDS

# 25. SERIES EVOLUTION FRAMEWORK

When a book invents a successful new element:

1. Classify as Book-Specific by default.

The Collection should grow sharper—not denser with gimmicks.
